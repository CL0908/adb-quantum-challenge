Decision summary · Kyrgyz Republic & Tajikistan bonds · CAREC pool · November 2026

Disaster Relief Bond Model Risk

The risk figures behind the bonds are uncertain mainly because of a few marginal inputs: the 1-in-50 rain level and the earthquake trigger rates. Cross-border dependence matters only in the far tail of a pool, and faster computation would not narrow any of these ranges. Better data would.

Built on Tan Chun Loong's RDPO model and its exact references (github.com/CL0908/adb-quantum-challenge), with parameter uncertainty taken from public data. Indicative: public proxies, not a re-pricing of the bonds. All probabilities are per three-year term.

What to decide

  • Price and report the tail metrics as ranges. Both bonds exhausted: 95% range 3.1 × 10−7 to 1.3 × 10−4 (central 2.2 × 10−5; brief 3.2 × 10−5). Expected loss of the two bonds: US$5.5m to US$19.2m (central US$10.1m).
  • Collect rain-extreme data for the two countries first. It carries 81% of the uncertainty in the chance that both bonds are exhausted. Expect it to narrow, not disappear: with about 46 years of index data, even an ideal addition narrows the 2% trigger interval only to about 0.65 of its width.
  • Then improve the earthquake catalogue and its magnitude–frequency slope. It carries 97% of the uncertainty in the two bonds' expected loss and 78% of the uncertainty in the CAREC pool's chance of losing more than US$240m.
  • Confirm the contract's event definition for aftershocks. If aftershocks pay as separate events, the chance of both bonds being exhausted rises 2.7-fold and expected loss 1.2-fold. Reading the contract settles this at no cost.
  • For a pool, watch shared earthquakes in the far tail. Their share of the uncertainty grows from 14% at a US$240m pool loss to 41% at US$500m. The strongest links in the catalogue are Afghanistan–Pakistan, Afghanistan–Tajikistan and Tajikistan–Kyrgyz Republic.
  • Stop spending on the shared-rain assumption. Station data put it near 2.7% (the brief assumes 25%), and at that level it carries under 2% of the uncertainty in every metric tested.

CData to collect, in order

  1. Rain extremes, Kyrgyz Republic and Tajikistan. Longer daily series and more gauges for the trigger index. Narrows the joint-exhaustion range (81% of its uncertainty for the two bonds, 56% inside the pool); knowing it exactly would cut the 95% interval by 56%.
  2. Earthquake catalogue and magnitude–frequency slope for the region. Narrows expected loss and the pool's exceedance figures; knowing it exactly would cut the expected-loss interval of the two bonds by 82%.
  3. Records of single earthquakes that reach two countries. Matters for the pool's far tail (41% at US$500m) and for joint exhaustion inside the pool (35%).
  4. The contract's event definition. Free to obtain; moves joint exhaustion by up to 2.7-fold.

Not worth further spending on current evidence: the shared-rain share (under 2% of uncertainty), and climate-state, trend or earthquake-cascade effects, none of which were statistically significant in the available records.

What these numbers cannot tell you

  • They use public proxies: USGS earthquakes 1965–2016, NOAA significant earthquakes 1900–2020, GHCN-Daily rain stations and EM-DAT losses 1990–2020. The bonds' actual trigger data would replace them.
  • Payout triggers depend on shaking at the exposure. Checked against USGS ShakeMaps (21 country–event cases), neither a 50 km magnitude rule nor peak shaking intensity matched the payout tier well (24% and 33% exact agreement), so footprint assumptions remain open until the trigger definition is known.
  • Warming is a scenario, not a forecast: at +1 °C the chance of both bonds being exhausted rises 1.6- to 2.1-fold. A 33-model climate ensemble disagreed with the station tails and could not narrow them.
  • Computation is not the constraint. The exact pool model evaluates in about one second; none of the faster algorithms tested, classical or quantum, would change the ranges above.
Method. Uncertain inputs are drawn from posterior distributions fitted to the public data above and passed through the RDPO repository's exact models. Variance shares are first-order Sobol indices of the log of each metric (4,096 joint draws for the two bonds, 512 for the pool, with bootstrap intervals); "removes" uses the standard value-of-information reduction for posteriors that shrink with record length. Every figure is reproducible from the add-on scripts voi.py and model_risk.py.
